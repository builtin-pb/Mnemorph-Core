# Prompt Writing

For new or substantively revised prompts, including ordinary requests to add a capability or handle another situation, Prompt Writing composes the instructions while its caller owns purpose and adoption. Read affected instructions, callers and consumer context before use.

Instructions must make decisions and results clear to consumers and human reviewers. Use Taste throughout: readability cannot repair a missing operation, and an unselected rule cannot help.

## Production

### Focus

Identify behavior, constraints, decisions and result. Separate requirements from interpretations; read shaping experience without treating an old elicitation failure as obsolete intent.

Inspect existing behavior and find the affected responsibility. Avoid redundant rules. Edit locally if that responsibility is clear; reorganize or rewrite if an addition would tangle or scatter it. Preserve requirements and useful content, not inherited wording.

Choose the rule, demonstration, interface or activation route the consumer will meet. State condition, action and result; check whether defaults or examples narrow the goal. [Reading budgets](../memory/memory.md#reading-budgets) expose growth, not semantic adequacy.

### Challenge

When a natural-language rule has a consequentially uncertain boundary, calibrate it with contrasting examples of what counts, what does not, and plausible near misses or valid alternatives. Explain the decision each should produce and why; use those distinctions to revise the general rule. Then try fresh uncued ordinary requests on both sides, beyond the calibration examples. Put examples in the prompt only when they help its consumer make the intended decision; titles, defaults, order and examples can narrow a broad rule. Compare different structures or scopes when word changes share the bias.

Challenge additions with a strong countercase and simpler arrangement. Check prohibitions, exceptions, example templates and unused outputs. Look for omitted useful behavior.

### Internalize

Trace new requirements through framing, rules, examples, authority, outputs and later use. Revise connected parts, preserve unaffected requirements and unresolved tensions, and reconcile caller, higher-priority and activated support instructions from the consumer's viewpoint.

Repair the likely reading. Reconstruct a responsibility when repairs share a premise or exceptions accumulate; leave unaffected passages intact.

### Measure

Read the whole revised prompt, including unchanged passages. Have a fresh agent compare a rewrite or compression with the prior text against every human requirement recorded in the prompt's shaping accounts, and weigh its findings; size limits never license dropping one. Record new requirements there with the user's words and date. Check human inspectability and trace caller, selected instruction and result. Source review and uncued cases reveal defects, not reliable behavior.

Use [Review](../scrutiny/review.md) for breadth, risk or structural uncertainty; use [Behavioral evidence](../scrutiny/behavioral-evidence.md) for uncertain elicitation. Observe representative decisions and results, with fresh uncued use when activation matters. Repair material defects when authorized and worth the cost. If repair is inappropriate, report the concrete consequence and reason; reporting alone cannot replace a warranted repair. Scores cannot excuse unreadability or lost requirements.

## Judgment

### Few

Seek the fewest rules, exceptions, examples, sections and files that preserve decisions. Delete or merge before adding; fix structure before shortening words. Count reader interpretation, repetition, navigation, retries and coordination. Prefer a scoped general rule to an exception list.

### Comprehensive

Give consequential decisions a condition, action and usable result, including defaults, authority, exceptions and stop behavior without preempting human choices. Name the plausible failure and alternative in a prohibition. Labels and pseudocode cannot substitute for an operation or recognizable result.

Make behavior inspectable through an observation, artifact, trace or execution without compulsory tests for every qualitative instruction. Give each requested record a consumer; keep private notes out of user artifacts unless needed. Catch-alls cannot replace missing decisions.

### Orthogonal

Use imperatives or “must” for requirements, “prefer” for defaults and “may” for permissions. Name distinct concepts distinctly; keep interacting conditions together and references unambiguous.

Give scope and intent prominence over rationale and format. State rules before examples; generalize taught operations and distinguish illustrations from required output. Give peers comparable space and keep support subordinate. Check whether outline order favors an easier, narrower reading.

### Modular

Use sections for substantial distinct responsibilities; keep short prompts simple. Keep dependent conditions with their rule and search callers for duplicate authority.

Put general behavior in the main prompt. Split support only when activation, reuse or revision repays navigation; callers must say when to use it and how its result changes work. Supply each prompt, tool, evaluator and handoff with needed inputs, authority and interfaces; define exact interfaces once and name who continues after partial or failed work.
